If you're a disabled veteran in Florida, you've probably heard you can stop paying property taxes, but it's hard to find a straight answer on who qualifies and what you have to do.

This guide covers the full exemption for veterans rated 100% permanent and total, what lower ratings get you, how to apply, and the rules that trip people up.

> **The short answer:** If the VA has certified your service-connected disability as **total and permanent**, Florida exempts your homestead from all ad valorem property taxes ([s. 196.081](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/196.081)).
>
> - A 100% rating alone isn't enough. The VA must certify the disability as **permanent and total**.
> - You still owe **non-ad valorem charges** on the same bill, such as solid waste, stormwater, and CDD fees.
> - Nothing happens automatically. You have to **apply with your county property appraiser**.

## Who Qualifies for the Full Exemption

To get the full exemption on your Florida home, you need all of the following:

- An **honorable discharge**.
- A **service-connected disability** that the VA or U.S. government has certified in writing as **total and permanent**.
- **Florida permanent residency** on January 1 of the tax year.
- **Ownership** of the home, which you use as your permanent residence (your homestead).

A similar full exemption applies to veterans who use a **wheelchair** because of a service-connected disability ([s. 196.091](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/196.091)). It has its own documentation rules, so check with your property appraiser.

**What about TDIU?** If you're paid at the 100% rate through **individual unemployability (TDIU)**, there's no statewide rule that says TDIU does or doesn't qualify. The test is whether your VA letter certifies your disability as **total and permanent**. Property appraisers decide case by case whether a letter meets that standard, a point the Florida Attorney General confirmed in a 2012 opinion. If your VA benefit summary letter says your disability is permanent and total, bring it to your county property appraiser.

## How to Apply (and the March 1 Deadline)

Florida's veteran exemptions are handled by your **county property appraiser**, not the VA. Here's the process:

1. **Get your documents.** You'll usually need your DD-214 (proof of honorable discharge) and a VA benefit summary letter showing your disability rating, plus permanent and total status if you're applying for the full exemption. Many counties want the letter to show that status as of January 1.
2. **File Form DR-501** with your county property appraiser. Most counties let you apply online. If you're also applying for the homestead exemption, it's the same form. The deployment exemption uses **Form DR-501M**.
3. **Meet the March 1 deadline** for that tax year. If you miss it, you may be able to file late until 25 days after your county mails its proposed tax notices (TRIM notices) in August, but approval depends on your circumstances.
4. **Watch for renewals.** After you're approved, your county mails a renewal each year by February 1, and in most counties the exemption renews automatically while you remain eligible. Tell the appraiser if your rating changes or you move.

**Waiting on the VA?** You can apply before your VA paperwork arrives. Once you provide it, the exemption takes effect from your original application date ([DOR PT-109](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt109.pdf)).

**Denied?** You can petition your county's value adjustment board to review the decision.

You can find your county's office in the [Florida Department of Revenue's property appraiser directory](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Pages/LocalOfficials.aspx).

## The Rules Most Veterans Don't Hear About

- **100% isn't always permanent and total.** A 100% rating that the VA expects to review later doesn't qualify for the full exemption on its own. You'd get the $5,000 exemption instead.
- **It only covers ad valorem taxes.** CDD fees, solid waste, stormwater, and other non-ad valorem charges on your bill are still owed. In newer communities, CDD fees can run hundreds or thousands of dollars a year.
- **January 1 sets the year.** Your residency and ownership on January 1 decide that year's exemption. If you buy mid-year, you'll owe that year's taxes, though the refund below can cover part of them.
- **It doesn't move with you.** If you sell and buy another Florida home, you have to apply again for the new one.
- **Rentals and second homes don't get the full exemption.** Only the $5,000 exemption can apply to property that isn't your homestead.

## The Prorated Refund for Mid-Year Buyers

If you're certified permanent and total and buy your Florida home between **January 1 and November 1**, you can get a refund of part of that year's ad valorem taxes ([s. 196.081(1)(b)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/196.081)). Since the 2024 tax roll, this applies even if you didn't have the exemption on another Florida home.

The refund covers the days from your closing date through December 31, counting the closing day. For example, closing on October 31 covers 62 of 365 days. It's paid after your exemption is granted for the following year, and you don't need to file a separate refund application ([DOR refund FAQ](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/FAQ_VPTDExemption.pdf)). Closings after November 1 don't qualify.

## Surviving Spouses

Florida's veteran exemptions can carry over to a surviving spouse:

- **Full exemption:** carries over to a surviving spouse who holds title to the home, lives there permanently, and doesn't remarry.
- **Death on active duty:** the surviving spouse of a service member who died from service-connected causes while on active duty may qualify for the full exemption.
- **65+ combat discount:** carries over to a spouse who holds title, lives in the home, and hasn't remarried. If the spouse sells, the discount can move to a new permanent residence.
- **$5,000 exemption:** may carry over under certain circumstances.

Surviving spouses apply through the county property appraiser with the veteran's documentation and proof of the spouse's status.

## Will 2026 Property Tax Changes Affect Veterans?

Florida voters decide [Amendment 3](/learn/florida-amendment-3-property-taxes-2026) on **November 3, 2026**. It would raise the homestead exemption from non-school taxes to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.

If you have the full exemption, you already pay no ad valorem tax on your homestead, so Amendment 3 wouldn't change your bill. If you have the $5,000 exemption or the 65+ combat discount, a larger homestead exemption would lower your non-school taxes further.

## Bottom Line for Florida Veterans

If the VA has certified your disability as permanent and total, your Florida homestead can be free of property taxes, but only after you apply with your county. Lower ratings still get real savings, especially for veterans 65 and older with combat-related disabilities.

Other benefits can help when you buy. Veterans who receive disability compensation don't pay the funding fee on a [VA loan in Florida](/home-loan/va-loan), and you may also qualify for [Florida housing grants for disabled homebuyers](/learn/florida-housing-grants-for-disabled). Older homeowners can also review [Florida property tax exemptions for seniors](/learn/florida-property-tax-exemptions-for-seniors).
