## Visa Options to Relocate to Florida from Canada

When [planning your move to Florida](/learn/cheapest-places-to-buy-house-in-florida) from Canada, the right visa depends on why you're moving: work, study, family, or investment.

### U.S. Work Visas for Canadian Citizens in Florida

For Canadian citizens seeking employment opportunities in Florida, several work visa categories are available:

- **TN Visa (USMCA Professional Work Permit)** - The TN classification was created under the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) and continues under the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA). It permits qualified Canadian professionals in listed occupations, such as accountants, engineers, and scientists, to work in Florida with a U.S. job offer. Canadians can usually apply at a U.S. port of entry.
- **H-1B Visa (Temporary Work Visa for Florida)** - The H-1B visa allows skilled Canadian professionals to work temporarily in various industries within Florida, typically for up to three years at a time and up to six years in total.
- **E-1/E-2 Visa Florida (Treaty Trader/Business Visa)** - For Canadians who carry on substantial trade with the U.S. or invest in a U.S. business, such as buying or starting one in Florida.
- **L-1 (Intra-Company Transfers for Florida)** - This visa is for Canadians already employed by a parent, branch, affiliate, or subsidiary company based outside the U.S. who intend to work for the same organization within Florida.
- **O-1 Visa (Extraordinary Skills Visa for Florida)** - For people with extraordinary ability in fields such as science, the arts, education, business, or athletics.

### Student Visas for Canadian Students Moving to Florida

If you're a Canadian student planning to study in Florida, you have two main options:

- **F-1 Visa** - For students enrolling in a college, university, or English language program in Florida.
- **M-1 Visa** - For vocational or technical programs, such as culinary arts or automotive mechanics.

### Family-Based Immigration U.S. Visa Options for Canadians Moving to Florida

If you have close family who are U.S. citizens or permanent residents, they may be able to sponsor you:

- **Marriage Visas for Florida** - Canadians engaged to a U.S. citizen can apply for a K-1 visa, often called a "fiancé visa."
- **Immediate Relative Visas for Florida** - For spouses, children, and parents of U.S. citizens, including IR-1 (spouse of a U.S. citizen), IR-2 (child of a U.S. citizen), and IR-5 (parent of a U.S. citizen) visas.
- **Family Preference Visas for Florida** - For other relatives of U.S. citizens and permanent residents, including F-2A (spouses and children of U.S. permanent residents), F-3 (married sons and daughters of U.S. citizens), and F-4 (brothers and sisters of U.S. citizens) visas.

### Immigration Opportunities Based on Investment in Florida

If you have significant capital to invest, the EB-5 program offers a green card through investment. The minimum investment is $800,000 in a targeted employment area or $1,050,000 elsewhere, and the investment must create at least 10 U.S. jobs. Buying a home for your own use does not qualify.

### Diversity Visa Lottery

The U.S. Diversity Visa (DV) lottery grants up to 55,000 green cards each year to people born in countries with historically low immigration to the U.S.

Canada is currently excluded from the lottery because so many Canadians already immigrate to the U.S. Eligibility is based on your country of birth, though, so a Canadian citizen born in an eligible country may still qualify. The list of eligible countries changes every year, so check the State Department's instructions before applying.

Keep in mind that immigration rules change often. An experienced U.S. immigration lawyer can confirm which option fits you and help you avoid delays.

Whichever visa you pursue, approval depends on submitting the right documents.

## Necessary Documents for Relocating from Canada to Florida

Having these documents ready will make your move from Canada to Florida much smoother:

- **Valid Passport** - Make sure your Canadian passport is valid for your entire planned stay in the U.S.
- **Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative (WHTI)-Compliant Travel Document** - If you're entering the U.S. by land or sea, you need a passport, passport card, NEXUS card, or another WHTI-compliant document.
- **Birth Certificate** - Used to verify your age, identity, and relationship to any family members applying with you.
- **Medical Examination** - Green card (immigrant visa) applicants are required to undergo a medical examination performed by an approved physician.
- **Photographs** - Most visa and green card applications require recent passport-style photos.
- **Financial Documentation** - Show that you can support yourself with bank statements, tax returns, pay stubs, and similar records.
- **Marriage Certificate** - For marriage-based visas, you'll need a certified copy of your marriage certificate.
- **Police Clearance Certificate** - Immigrant visa applicants generally need police certificates from countries where they've lived for more than 12 months.
- **Additional Documentation** - Depending on the specific visa category, you might need supplementary documents such as proof of educational or professional qualifications, a U.S. job offer letter, or an affidavit of support from a sponsor.

U.S. immigration officials will review these documents to decide whether you qualify.

Make sure everything is accurate and complete, since errors or missing documents can delay your application.

If you want to live in Florida permanently, look into the green card category that fits your situation.

A green card makes you a permanent resident and opens up benefits like standard U.S. mortgage programs.

## Snowbird Rules: How Long Canadians Can Stay in Florida

Many Canadians spend the winter in Florida without moving permanently. Here are the rules to know:

- **Up to six months per visit** - Canadian visitors don't need a visa and are usually admitted for up to six months at a time, at the border officer's discretion. Visitor status doesn't allow you to work in the U.S.
- **The substantial presence test** - The IRS counts all of your days in the U.S. this year, plus one-third of last year's days and one-sixth of the days from the year before. If the total reaches 183, you may be treated as a U.S. tax resident.
- **Form 8840** - Snowbirds who meet the test but keep closer ties to Canada can usually avoid U.S. tax residency by filing IRS Form 8840, the Closer Connection Exemption Statement, by June 15 each year.
- **Registration for longer stays** - Since April 2025, Canadians who stay in the U.S. 30 days or longer and don't already have a U.S. registration record may need to register online with USCIS. Check current USCIS and CBP rules before each trip.

Owning a Florida home doesn't change these limits, but it does give you a place to come back to every winter.

## Buying a Home in Florida as a Canadian

Canadians are among the most active foreign buyers of U.S. homes. Canadian buyers made up 14% of all foreign buyers of existing U.S. homes from April 2024 to March 2025, and Florida remains the top destination for international buyers, drawing 21% of them (National Association of Realtors, 2025).

- **You can buy without living here** - Canadians can buy houses, condos, and land anywhere in Florida, and you don't need a visa, green card, or U.S. address to own property. Florida's 2023 foreign ownership law (SB 264) restricts buyers from certain countries, but it does not apply to Canadians.
- **You can finance without U.S. credit** - A [foreign national loan](/home-loan/foreign-national-loan) lets Canadians buy with no U.S. credit score or Social Security number. Expect about 25%–30% down, proof of income and assets, and several months of reserves.
- **Green card holders have more options** - Once you have permanent residence and U.S. credit, you can qualify for [conventional loans](/learn/conventional-mortgages-in-florida) and [FHA loans](/home-loan/fha-loan) with much smaller down payments.
- **Condos need extra review** - Many snowbirds buy condos, and lenders review the building as well as the buyer. See our [Florida condo loan guide](/home-loan/condo-loan) before you make an offer.
- **Planning to rent it out?** - If you'll rent your Florida home while you're in Canada, a [short-term rental DSCR loan](/home-loan/dscr-loan/short-term-rental) qualifies you on the property's rental income.
- **Know the closing costs** - On a financed purchase, Florida charges documentary stamp tax of 0.35% and intangible tax of 0.2% on the mortgage, plus title insurance and lender fees.
- **Plan for the exchange rate** - Your down payment and monthly payments are in U.S. dollars, so currency swings can change your real cost.

## How to Apply for a Green Card as a Canadian Citizen?

The process of obtaining a green card as a Canadian citizen follows the same rules as for any other foreign national.

The basic steps look like this:

### Step 1 - Determine Your Eligibility

First, figure out which green card category you qualify for.

The main options are family-based green cards (immediate relatives and the F-1 through F-4 preference categories) or employment-based green cards (EB-1, EB-2, EB-3, and the EB-5 investor category).

### Step 2 - Complete Application Forms and Submit Documentation

Next, complete the application forms for your category.

Include supporting documents such as a valid passport, birth certificate, marriage certificate (if applicable), financial records, results of a medical examination, and any other documents requested by U.S. immigration officials.

### Step 3 - Await Approval and Receive Your Green Card

After you submit your application, you'll wait for a decision. If it's approved, you'll receive your green card.

Upon acquiring your green card, you keep your Canadian citizenship and become a U.S. lawful permanent resident. As a permanent resident, you can live and work anywhere in the U.S. and enjoy most of the rights of U.S. citizens, although you can't vote in federal elections until you naturalize.

It also makes you eligible for Florida's homestead property tax savings once your Florida home is your permanent residence.

## Florida's Tax Advantages

[Becoming a resident of Florida](/learn/requirements-to-buy-a-house-in-florida) comes with real tax benefits.

Here's what you can expect as a Florida resident:

- **No State Income Tax -** Florida has no personal state income tax, so you keep more of what you earn.
- **Retirement Benefits -** With no state tax on Social Security, pensions, or retirement account withdrawals, Florida is one of the most retiree-friendly states.
- **Property Tax Exemptions -** Permanent residents who make Florida home their primary residence can claim the [homestead exemption](/learn/florida-property-tax-exemptions-for-seniors), which lowers taxable value and caps annual assessment increases. Snowbirds on visitor status don't qualify.
- **No Inheritance or Estate Tax -** Florida does not impose a state inheritance or estate tax. U.S. federal estate tax can still apply to Florida property owned by Canadians, but the Canada-U.S. tax treaty provides relief for many owners.
- **Business-Friendly -** No personal income tax also makes Florida popular with business owners and self-employed people.

Before you leave Canada for good, talk to a cross-border tax advisor about Canada's departure tax and your U.S. filing obligations, so there are no surprises.
