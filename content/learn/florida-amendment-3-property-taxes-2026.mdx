> **Status (updated November 4, 2026):** Florida voters **approved Amendment 3** on November 3, 2026, with about **62%** of the vote in unofficial results, above the 60% it needed. It takes effect **January 1, 2027**, once the state certifies the results.

If you're planning to buy a home in Florida, Amendment 3 just lowered one of the biggest costs of owning it.

This guide explains what the new homestead exemption means for buyers: your property taxes, your monthly payment, how much home you can afford, and when it makes sense to buy.

It also covers the December 31, 2026 residency rule for people moving to Florida, plus what the change does and doesn't do for condos, second homes, and rentals.

## What Amendment 3 Means for Homebuyers at a Glance

- **Lower property taxes on the home you live in.** The homestead exemption from non-school taxes rises from $51,411 today to **$150,000 in 2027** and **$250,000 in 2028**, then adjusts for inflation.
- **A smaller monthly payment, or more home for the same payment.** Most mortgage payments include property taxes, so lower taxes free up room in your budget.
- **Possibly easier qualifying.** Lenders count property taxes in your debt-to-income ratio.
- **Timing matters.** Owning and living in the home on January 1, 2027 gets you the new exemption on your 2027 tax bill.
- **Moving to Florida?** Becoming a permanent resident by December 31, 2026 gets you the full exemption. Arriving later means a smaller exemption for your first four years.
- **School taxes, insurance, and HOA dues don't change.** Neither does the need to apply for homestead after you buy.

## Your Property Taxes and Monthly Payment

Here's what Amendment 3 does to the tax bill on a **$400,000 home** you live in, using a 1% non-school tax rate and a 0.6% school tax rate. Rates vary by county and city, so treat this as an illustration.

| | 2026 rules | 2027 | 2028 and after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Homestead exemption, non-school taxes | $51,411 | $150,000 | $250,000 |
| Non-school taxes | $3,486 | $2,500 | $1,500 |
| School taxes | $2,250 | $2,250 | $2,250 |
| **Total property taxes per year** | **$5,736** | **$4,750** | **$3,750** |
| **Property taxes per month** | **$478** | **$396** | **$313** |

By 2028, that's about **$1,986 a year, or $165 a month, less** for the same home.

If your taxes are paid through escrow, your mortgage payment drops after your servicer's next escrow analysis picks up the lower bill. Your principal and interest stay the same.

*Assumes assessed value equals the purchase price and no other exemptions, special assessments, or fees. The school exemption stays at $25,000. Your property appraiser and tax collector set your actual values and bill.*

## How Much More Home You Could Afford

Lower taxes leave more of your monthly budget for the mortgage itself. Suppose you can spend about **$2,857 a month** on principal, interest, taxes, and insurance:

| | $400,000 home, 2026 rules | Same home, 2028 exemption | Higher price, 2028 exemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home price / assessed value | $400,000 | $400,000 | $424,864 |
| Down payment (20%) | $80,000 | $80,000 | $84,973 |
| Principal and interest | $2,128.97 | $2,128.97 | $2,261.31 |
| Property taxes | $477.99 | $312.50 | $345.65 |
| Homeowners insurance | $250.00 | $250.00 | $250.00 |
| **Monthly total** | **$2,856.96** | **$2,691.47** | **$2,856.96** |

At the same monthly budget, the math supports a price about **$24,864 higher**, roughly $425,000, with about $4,973 more down. Or you could keep the $400,000 home and pay about $165 less a month.

*Hypothetical illustration, not a rate quote or pre-approval: 30-year fixed at 7%, 20% down, no mortgage insurance, the tax rates above, and $250 a month for insurance. Assumes you qualify for homestead. Figures are rounded.*

## Will It Help You Qualify for a Mortgage?

Lenders count your estimated property taxes in your monthly housing payment and your debt-to-income ratio. Lower taxes leave more room under the lender's limits, which can help buyers who are close to the line, especially with FHA and first-time buyer programs where every dollar of the payment counts.

The catch is how your lender estimates taxes on a home you haven't homesteaded yet. Some lenders use the seller's current tax bill, which may include a different exemption or none at all. Others estimate from the purchase price and local tax rates, with or without the homestead exemption you'll file for. Ask your loan officer which method they use, because it can change your approval amount by thousands of dollars.

If you're using down payment assistance such as [Florida Hometown Heroes](/hometown-heroes), the lower tax estimate also helps your full payment fit the program's limits. See our [Florida first-time homebuyer grants and programs guide](/learn/first-time-homebuyer/grants-and-programs) for the options in your county.

## Should You Buy Now or Wait?

Amendment 3 doesn't give buyers a reason to wait, and it gives some a reason to move sooner.

- **If you already live in Florida**, you get the larger exemption whenever you buy, so there's no penalty for buying now or later.
- **To get the exemption on your 2027 tax bill**, you need to own the home and live in it on **January 1, 2027**, then apply by **March 1, 2027**. Close in February 2027 instead, and your first homestead year is 2028, so you'd pay 2027 taxes with no homestead exemption. On our $400,000 example, that's about **$1,650 more** for 2027.
- **Prices could rise.** Lower ownership costs can let buyers offer more, and research on a property tax break in Philadelphia found the savings were partly reflected in sale prices. That doesn't predict Florida prices, but it's a reason not to count on waiting.
- **Your rate, the home, and your budget still matter most.** A tax exemption shouldn't push you into a home you can't afford for other reasons.

Most purchases take 30 to 45 days from contract to closing, so owning by January 1 usually means getting pre-approved now and having an accepted offer by late November.

## Moving to Florida? The December 31, 2026 Rule

This is the part of Amendment 3 that matters most for out-of-state buyers. The amendment gives the larger exemption to homeowners who **maintained a permanent residence in Florida as of December 31, 2026**:

- **Florida residents by December 31, 2026:** the full $150,000 (2027) and $250,000 (2028) non-school exemption once you buy and qualify for homestead, even if you buy years later.
- **New residents from January 1, 2027:** a $50,000 non-school exemption plus $25,000 for school taxes until your **fifth year** of homestead, then the full amount.
- **Local option:** starting in 2030, a county or city can shorten the five-year wait for a critical local need by a two-thirds vote.

On a $400,000 home, arriving after the deadline costs about **$2,000 a year for four years**, or roughly **$8,000**.

The date is about **residency, not buying**. If you're relocating soon, establishing Florida residency before December 31 (a Florida driver's license, vehicle registration, voter registration, and a lease or home in your name) can preserve the full exemption even if you buy in 2027. Your county property appraiser makes the final call, the Legislature still has to set procedures, and the residency rule applies "to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution," so it could be challenged. Keep records of when you moved.

## Condos, Second Homes, and Investment Properties

- **Condos you live in** get the same larger homestead exemption as a house. Building reserves, special assessments, and insurance are still the bigger cost questions for Florida condo buyers; our [Florida condo loan guide](/home-loan/condo-loan) covers what lenders review.
- **Second homes and vacation homes** don't get a homestead exemption. Starting in 2027, their assessed value for non-school taxes can rise no more than **5% a year** instead of 10%, after it resets to market value at purchase.
- **Rentals and investment properties** with nine units or fewer get the same 5% cap. It limits how fast the taxable value grows, not today's bill. Investors can run the numbers with our [Florida DSCR loan calculator](/florida-dscr-loan-calculator).

## What Doesn't Change for Buyers

- **School taxes.** Only the first $25,000 of assessed value is exempt from school taxes, so you'll still pay them.
- **The purchase reset.** Your home is assessed at market value after you buy. The Save Our Homes cap of 3% a year (or inflation, if lower) applies after that.
- **Insurance, HOA dues, and fees.** Homeowners insurance, flood insurance, association dues, and non-ad valorem assessments aren't affected.
- **Local taxes could shift.** Counties and cities lose revenue under Amendment 3, nearly $5 billion statewide in 2027-28 by one legislative estimate, so tax rates and fees may change.

## After You Buy: Claim Your Homestead Exemption

The new exemption isn't automatic. After closing:

1. **Move in** and make the home your permanent residence by **January 1** of the tax year.
2. **Apply for homestead** with your county property appraiser by **March 1**. Most counties accept applications online.
3. **Have ready** your deed or recorded closing documents, a Florida driver's license or ID at the property address, Florida vehicle registration, voter registration if you're registered, and Social Security numbers for owners and spouses.
4. **Moving within Florida?** Apply for Save Our Homes portability at the same time to carry part of your capped savings to the new home.
