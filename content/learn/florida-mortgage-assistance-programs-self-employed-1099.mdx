Self-employed and 1099 borrowers in Florida can get a mortgage without two years of perfect tax returns. **Bank statement loans, 1099-only loans, and P&L loans** qualify you on the income your business actually brings in — not the lower number left after write-offs.

This guide compares every realistic option, shows how lenders calculate self-employed income, and lists what you'll need to apply.

## Who Does Self-Employed Mortgages in Florida?

Most banks only offer conventional and FHA loans, which rely on your tax returns. Programs like bank statement and 1099 loans come from **non-QM lenders**, and mortgage brokers can shop several of them at once.

## Self-Employed Mortgage Options Compared

| Loan Type | Income Verified By | Typical Down | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank statement | 12–24 months of bank statements | 10%–20% | Owners with heavy write-offs |
| 1099-only | 1–2 years of 1099s | 10%–20% | Contractors and gig workers |
| P&L-only | A prepared profit-and-loss statement | 15%–20% | Clean books, complex returns |

| DSCR | The property's rent | 20%–25% | Investment properties only |
| Conventional / FHA | 1–2 years of tax returns | 3%–3.5% | Returns that show enough income |

Non-QM programs typically look for credit scores in the 620–680+ range, while FHA starts lower. Ranges vary by lender and change with the market — your credit, down payment, and reserves decide where you land.

## Bank Statement Loans

A bank statement loan is the most popular option for self-employed Floridians. Instead of tax returns, the lender reviews **12 or 24 months of bank statements** and averages your deposits.

- **Personal statements:** most eligible deposits count as income, excluding transfers between your own accounts.
- **Business statements:** the lender applies an expense factor — often around 50% — unless your CPA or tax preparer documents a lower one.
- **Example:** $15,000 in average monthly business deposits × 50% = **$7,500 a month** in qualifying income.

Expect to show 1–2 years of self-employment, a business license or CPA letter, and cash reserves after closing.

## 1099-Only Loans

Built for independent contractors, real estate agents, rideshare and delivery drivers, and other 1099 earners. The lender qualifies you on **1 to 2 years of 1099 forms** — no tax returns or W-2s. Many programs apply a small expense factor to your gross 1099 income, and some count it in full.

## P&L-Only Loans

If your books are clean but your tax returns are complicated, some programs accept a **profit-and-loss statement prepared by a CPA, enrolled agent, or tax preparer**, sometimes alongside two or three months of bank statements.

## Asset-Depletion Loans

No steady income? Asset-depletion (or asset-utilization) loans turn your **savings, investments, and retirement accounts** into qualifying income by dividing eligible assets over a set number of months. They're common for early retirees, investors, and business owners between ventures.

## Conventional and FHA Loans With Tax Returns

If your tax returns show enough income, a conventional or [FHA loan](/home-loan/fha-loan) will usually give you the lowest rate and down payment.

- **Two years of returns** is standard, though some approvals accept one.
- **Self-employed less than two years?** Conventional loans can accept 12–24 months of self-employment with prior experience in the same field.
- **Freddie Mac Home Possible** allows 3% down for borrowers under its income limits.
- **Florida down payment assistance** — including [Hometown Heroes](/hometown-heroes) — works with these loans for eligible full-time Florida workers. Self-employed borrowers should confirm they meet the program's employment and income rules.

## Buying a Rental? Use a DSCR Loan

If the home is an investment property, you may not need to document personal income at all. A [DSCR loan](/home-loan/dscr-loan) qualifies on the property's rent, which makes it a favorite for self-employed investors. Our [Florida DSCR calculator](/florida-dscr-loan-calculator) shows whether a property's rent covers its payment.

## Documents Self-Employed Borrowers Need

- Government-issued ID
- 12–24 months of bank statements (bank statement loans), 1099 forms (1099 loans), or a prepared P&L (P&L loans)
- Proof you've been self-employed — a business license, CPA letter, or state filing
- Two months of statements for your down payment and reserves
- Tax returns, only if you choose a conventional or FHA loan

## Florida Tips for Self-Employed Buyers

- **Budget for insurance.** Florida homeowners and flood insurance can be costly, and the lender counts it in your debt-to-income ratio.
- **Separate your accounts.** Mixing personal and business deposits makes bank statements harder to underwrite. Keep them apart for at least a few months before you apply.
- **Plan your refinance.** Many buyers start with a bank statement loan and refinance into a conventional loan once their returns support it.
